About

Newtown, Sydney NSW

Hi, call me Ahammed. I build AI tools for a living, and in my spare time I build more AI tools for fun. Agentic coding, RAG systems, voice interfaces, automations that get software to talk to software that was never meant to talk, this is the stuff I can't stop thinking about. When new AI features drop, I'm the person flashing an old laptop with Linux at 11pm to try them out.

I spent seven and a half years at a Sydney startup called Pitch Pocket, doing whatever the business needed next. I started by cleaning phone number lists. A year or two later my boss asked if I could build a mobile app. I'd never built one, so I said yes and taught myself on YouTube. That app ended up becoming the company's core product. The pattern never really changed, something needed doing, I figured out how to do it, and over seven years I ended up running enterprise accounts for brands like Nespresso, PPG Paints, and Unilever, and eventually shipping an AI-powered SaaS platform on my own.

What I actually believe: AI is a superpower for small and mid-size businesses. Not because it replaces people but because it takes the draining, repetitive parts of a job and gets them out of the way. One person who can suddenly do more, without being stretched thinner, is a genuine competitive advantage. That's what I'm trying to build for the teams I work with.

Genie

Talk to a document. Watch it become a webpage.

You upload a document. You talk to it. A webpage appears. That's genuinely the whole idea. Upload your product brief, say out loud "make me a pricing page that feels clean and confident," and watch the page get built in front of you. No typing. No templates. No clicking around a page builder.

Ongoing personal experiment

The voice part was harder than it sounds. Browser audio cuts out after a few seconds, so I built around that with chunked recording and live transcription via OpenAI Whisper. You can speak continuously and the system holds on.

Grounding was the other hard problem. Without careful architecture, the model confidently fills in gaps. So I built a retrieval system on ChromaDB and Sentence Transformers that chunks your uploaded material, scores relevance, and only lets the model generate from what you actually provided.

On top of that, a custom MCP-style pipeline that classifies intent, expands vague voice queries into targeted vector searches, and routes tasks to the most cost-effective model. Plus a post-generation validator that audits HTML and injects Tailwind animations, GPU optimisations, and ARIA accessibility without another LLM call.

Project Muse

An AI that actually remembers you, and runs entirely on your own machine.

Most AI tools treat every conversation like it's the first one in the begining. Project Muse was my attempt at the opposite, an AI companion that runs entirely on your machine, builds a picture of you over time, and adapts how it talks to you based on what it learns. While you're not using it, it's working. A background engine reads through your past conversations, notices patterns in how you think, and quietly updates its model of you.

Ongoing personal experiment

Core architecture is an asynchronous AgentCoordinator that routes user input to specialised domain agents (Psychology, Philosophy, Literature, Cinema). The coordinator decides whether a single-agent or synthesised multi-agent response better fits the context.

The real novelty is the RuminationEngine, a background process that runs during downtime, analyses queued interactions, extracts emotional indicators and topic preferences, and updates a persistent psyche profile modelled on the Big Five (OCEAN).

Everything runs 100% locally. Ollama for LLM inference (Qwen models), ChromaDB for vector memory. Zero data leaves the machine. A RAG system grounds the AI's identity by pulling from curated literary texts (Oscar Wilde-inspired, because why not). And a Three.js/WebGL frontend visualises agent activity in real-time 3D.
